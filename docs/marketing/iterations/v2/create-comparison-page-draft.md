Read-it-later apps have been around for years. They promise a clean, organized space to catch up on your reading. But the way we actually work with information has evolved — saving an article is just the start. What you do _after_ you save it is what determines whether you learn from it or forget it.

Most read-it-later apps stop at saving. **Reader** doesn't. It's a personal research workspace: capture, annotate, chat with the content using AI, and export your thinking — all in one place.

## What sets Reader apart

### 1. Annotation that feeds your thinking

Highlights are a start. Reader lets you attach notes to any highlight, building a layer of _your_ thinking on top of the source material. Those annotated notes are then searchable and exportable — they don't live and die inside a closed app.

Pocket's free plan caps highlights at three per article. Instapaper highlights are unlimited but notes are shallow. Reader puts no limit on either.

### 2. AI that works on your actual content

Reader's AI features aren't bolted on — they're integrated into the reading workflow:

- **Article summary**: one-click summary of any saved article, so you can triage your reading queue faster.
- **AI chat (NotesAIChat)**: ask questions about the article and your own notes. Great for surfacing connections you missed on first read.
- **Research brief**: generate a structured brief from a set of articles and your annotations. Useful for writers, researchers, and anyone preparing a presentation.

Pocket has no AI. Instapaper has no AI. Readwise Reader has some AI, but its chat is limited to highlights, not your own notes.

### 3. Session review — actually remember what you read

Reader includes a spaced-recall session review that surfaces your past annotations at intervals. This is the gap between saving articles and actually learning from them. Browser bookmarks and basic read-it-later apps have no answer to this at all.

### 4. PDF as a first-class format

Many research workflows live in PDFs: papers, reports, contracts. Reader renders PDFs with the same annotation experience as articles. Pocket and Instapaper don't support PDF annotation at all.

### 5. Kanban board for your reading queue

Reader includes a board view to manage articles by status — reading, done, research, backlog. No other read-it-later app offers this without a separate tool like Notion or Trello.

## When to choose something else

We'd rather be honest than oversell:

- **Pocket** is fine if you want a casual read-later queue and already pay for a Pocket Premium subscription. The social discovery features are a bonus Pocket has and Reader doesn't.
- **Instapaper** is a solid minimalist reader. If you want the simplest possible interface and don't need AI or PDF support, Instapaper works.
- **Readwise Reader** is Reader's closest direct competitor. If you're already inside the Readwise ecosystem (with highlights synced to Notion/Obsidian), staying there may make more sense. Reader's edge: research briefs, board view, and no subscription lock-in on core annotation features.
- **Browser bookmarks** work if you're saving things to never read again.
